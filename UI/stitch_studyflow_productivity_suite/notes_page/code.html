Cellular respiration is a set of metabolic reactions and processes that take place in the cells of organisms to convert biochemical energy from nutrients into adenosine triphosphate (ATP), and then release waste products.

The Three Main Stages

  • Glycolysis: Occurs in the cytoplasm. Breaks down glucose into two molecules of pyruvate. Yields 2 ATP and 2 NADH.
  • Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle): Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Pyruvate is oxidized, producing CO2, NADH, FADH2, and 2 ATP.
  • Electron Transport Chain: Occurs in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Uses electrons from NADH and FADH2 to create a proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis. Yields ~34 ATP.

The overall chemical equation for aerobic cellular respiration is:

C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP
